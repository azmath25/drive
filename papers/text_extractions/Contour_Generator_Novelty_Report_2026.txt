 Executive assessment: The supplied manuscript contains mathematically defensible statements, but its
 original Willmore inequality is an elementary consequence of a coarea identity, pointwise ellipse estimates,
 Cauchy–Schwarz, and Gauss–Bonnet. There is extensive prior work on averaged silhouettes,
 Cauchy/Minkowski projection formulas, and curvature integrals. We did not find the exact unprojected
 formula stated verbatim in the targeted searches; absence from these searches does not imply novelty. The
 best opportunity is a genuinely nontrivial, sharp fixed-genus optimization or stability result.

 1. Mathematical object and the claims under review
 For a smooth, compact, boundaryless oriented surface S in three-dimensional Euclidean space, set Γ(u) = {p
 in S : N(p)·u = 0}. The mean spatial length of the complete (possibly hidden) contour generators is L =
 (1/4π)∫     length(Γ(u)) du. Write A for area, W = ∫ H² dA for the Willmore energy with H = (κ₁+κ₂)/2, and χ for
        S²                                              S
 Euler characteristic.

                                                 L² ≤ (A/2)(W − πχ)

 The original claim is sharp globally because spheres attain equality. The exact coarea representation is L =
 (1/4π)∫ ∫₀²π √(κ₁²cos²θ + κ₂²sin²θ) dθ dA. It is a natural instance of the coarea formula for a Gauss-map
        S
 incidence relation, which significantly reduces the expected novelty of the derivation.


 2. Closest and most consequential prior art
 [1] R. E. Miles (1975): An explicit derivation of Minkowski-type mean-projection identities for convex sets.
 In particular, random line-projection lengths are expressed through total mean curvature. This puts convex
 projected-contour formulas squarely within classical integral geometry, although they are not literally the
 same as an unprojected rim-length formula.

 [2] M. McGuire (2004): Expected silhouette-edge counts of polyhedral models are related to sums of
 dihedral angles. This provides early and direct evidence that silhouette expectations over directions are
 established research objects.

 [3] M. Glisse and S. Lazard (2008): An Upper Bound on the Average Size of Silhouettes, Discrete &
 Computational Geometry 40, 241–257. The work studies average silhouette lengths in the setting of
 nonconvex surfaces and establishes expected O(√n) mesh-silhouette complexity under geometric
 approximation hypotheses. The paper distinguishes transparent spatial rims and projected silhouettes, and
 uses average silhouette length explicitly. It is strong contextual prior art, not an identified publication of
 the exact inequality at issue.

 [4] S. Plantinga and G. Vegter (2003): Established smooth contour-generator terminology, projected
 apparent contours, generic singularities, and robust contour algorithms.

 [5] I. Izmestiev (2022): A published survey of mixed volumes and convex inequalities, including the
 proportionality of total mean curvature and mean width and classical Cauchy/Crofton identities. The convex
 case requires a careful comparison to this standard theory.

 4. A critical false proposal and a correct sharp replacement
 The proposed genus-independent inequality L² ≤ AW/4 is false; likewise the supporting suggestion ∫|H| dA ≥
 2L is false even among convex bodies. A long smoothly rounded cylinder provides explicit asymptotics.

 For a cylinder of radius r and lateral length h (smoothly rounded ends of fixed geometry), as h → ∞:

                                   L ~ 2h ;    A ~ 2πrh ;          W ~ πh/(2r).

 Therefore 4L²/(AW) → 16/π² ≈ 1.62114 > 1. The candidate c(0) = 1 is disproved even for convex genus-zero
 surfaces.

 Provable convex replacement: for nonnegative principal curvatures, the curvature-ellipse perimeter obeys
 P(κ₁,κ₂) ≤ 4(κ₁ + κ₂), since √(a²cos²θ + b²sin²θ) ≤ a|cosθ| + b|sinθ|. Consequently L ≤ (2/π)∫H dA and
 Cauchy–Schwarz yields

                                          L² ≤ (4/π²) AW (convex S).

 The long rounded-cylinder family makes the constant 4/π² sharp within the convex class. This is a useful
 correction, but its proof uses elementary estimates; a separate prior-art check is needed before calling the
 exact coefficient new.


 5. A better-formulated extremal programme
 For each nonnegative integer g, define the dimensionless extremal constant over connected smooth
 embedded closed surfaces of genus g:

                          c(g) := sup { 4L(S)² / [A(S)W(S)] : genus(S)=g }.

 The sphere gives ratio 1. The rounded-cylinder family gives c(0) ≥ 16/π² ≈ 1.62114. A ring torus with
 major/minor radii (R,r)=(2,1), based on the manuscript’s numerically integrated contour length L≈28.233659
 and exact A=8π² and W=4π²/√3, gives c(1) ≥ 1.77176. These are lower bounds, not exact values.

 The original inequality gives 4L²/(AW) ≤ 2[1 + 2π(g−1)/W]. Thus c(0), c(1) ≤ 2; and for g ≥ 1, W ≥ 4π gives
 c(g) ≤ g+1. None is known here to be optimal.
