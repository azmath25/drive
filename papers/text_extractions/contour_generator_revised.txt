                                                 Abstract
     For a smooth compact boundaryless oriented surface Σ ⊂ R3 and a unit viewing direction u,
     let Γu = {p ∈ Σ : ⟨N (p), u⟩ = 0} be its orthographic contour generator. We give a complete
     coarea argument for an exact formula for the direction-averaged spatial length L(Σ) in terms of
     the image of the unit tangent circle under the ∫ shape  operator. This implies the sharp estimate
     L(Σ)2 ≤ A(Σ)2
                    (W(Σ)  −  πχ(Σ)),  where  W   =   H 2
                                                          dA and H = (κ1 + κ2 )/2. Equality on a con-
     nected surface holds exactly for round spheres. We also derive an analogous exact formula for
     projected contour length counted with multiplicity, and give comparisons and scope qualifica-
     tions. The original bound is from the supplied note; the projected-length statements and equality
     analysis are explicitly identified as additions made during the mathematical audit. No claim of
     bibliographic novelty is made.

Keywords: contour generator; coarea formula; Gauss map; principal curvature; Willmore energy;
silhouette length.

2    Generic directions and the exact spatial-length formula
Lemma 2.1 (Generic regularity). The set of u ∈ S2 for which 0 is not a regular value of p 7→ hN (p), ui
has spherical measure zero. For every other u, Γu is a compact smooth one-dimensional submanifold
(possibly empty).

Proof. Consider F : Σ × S2 → R, F (p, u) = hN (p), ui, and set M = F −1 (0). At (p, u) ∈ M , the vector
N (p) belongs to Tu S2 ; hence
                               du F (p, u)[N (p)] = hN (p), N (p)i = 1.
Thus 0 is a regular value of F , and M is a compact smooth three-manifold. Consider the smooth
projection πu : M → S2 , (p, u) 7→ u. Sard’s theorem shows that the critical values of πu form a set
of spherical measure zero. For any regular value u, the fiber πu−1 (u) is a smooth one-dimensional
submanifold and is naturally identified with Γu . Equivalently, the differential of p 7→ F (p, u) does
not vanish along the zero set.

A round sphere of radius r has Γu a great circle of length 2πr for every u; also A = 4πr2 , W = 4π
and χ = 2. Thus equality holds and the constant is optimal.
Conversely, suppose equality holds. Equality in the Cauchy–Schwarz step forces |B| ≡ c on all of
Σ; equality in the integrated Jensen estimate forces κ21 = κ22 at every point (the deficit is contin-
uous and nonnegative). We must have c > 0: otherwise dN = 0 everywhere, so each connected
component is contained in a plane, impossible for a compact boundaryless immersed surface. Con-
sequently on each connected component K ∈ {c2 /2, −c2 /2}, and continuity forces a constant sign.
If K < 0 on a component, κ1 = −κ2 there and H ≡ 0. This would be a compact boundaryless
minimal surface in R3 , which is impossible: each coordinate function of the immersion would be
harmonic and hence constant. Therefore K > 0, κ1 = κ2 on each component, and the component
is totally umbilic with constant nonzero principal curvature. It is a round sphere. Since |B| = c
globally, all radii coincide. Conversely, such a union attains equality by direct substitution.

Remark 4.2 (What is—and is not—a visible contour). A visible silhouette from an opaque surface
consists of some of the projected rim points; its image-set length is no greater than the length of
all parametrized projected rim branches. Thus, whenever visibility is defined in the usual ortho-
graphic setting and ℓvis (u) denotes the one-dimensional Hausdorff length of the visible silhouette,
ℓvis (u) ≤ ℓpr (u) ≤ ℓ(u) for almost every u. Theorem 3.2 consequently bounds average visible sil-
houette length from above, but (10) is not an exact formula for that visible length when occlusion
occurs. For a round sphere all three quantities agree for every view.


5   Checks, interpretation, and scope

Sphere check
For a round sphere of radius r, |κ1 | = |κ2 | = 1/r, so PB = 2π/r. Theorem 2.2 gives L = 2πr. Its
projected contours are great circles contained in the projection plane, so Lpr = 2πr as well. Theo-
rem 3.2 is an equality.

Interpretation and limitations
The upper bound is dimensionally consistent: L has units of length; A has units of length squared;
and W and χ are dimensionless. It controls the average, not the maximum over viewing directions.
It does not impose a bound in terms of area and genus alone; the curvature energy is essential.
The projected identity measures all projected rim branches with multiplicity, whereas an image’s
outer silhouette can exclude occluded branches. Finally, since the proof uses coarea, a pointwise
ellipse estimate, Cauchy–Schwarz and Gauss–Bonnet, novelty should not be inferred from validity
or sharpness alone.
